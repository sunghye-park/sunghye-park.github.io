Cancerdagboken

A needs analysis for cancer patient support, done with KI Innovations AB and oncologists at Karolinska Institutet, seen through the eyes of contact nurses.

Role
UX researcher
Team
4 UX design students, Nackademin UX25
Duration
6 weeks, Sep. to Oct. 2025
Client
KI Innovations AB, with oncologists at Karolinska Institutet
Output
A needs-analysis report presented to KI Innovations AB
Project Index

01

The problem

A cancer patient's care journey is often long and fragmented, with many contacts, treatments and appointments that can be hard to keep track of. We were commissioned to complement earlier work with the perspective of healthcare staff, specifically contact nurses (kontaktsjuksköterskor).

Scope, as defined by the brief

Mapping needs only, not building a solution. Developing a communication channel between patients and staff was explicitly out of scope. The brief asked us to let the interviews surface needs, rather than starting from a ready-made solution.

02

Method

We started with desk research (omvärldsbevakning) to explore existing digital tools and support in cancer care. We then planned in-depth interviews with contact nurses and added a survey to gather more perspectives. Both focused on the challenges nurses observe in patients' daily lives, needs that healthcare cannot always meet, and factors that affect whether support works in practice.

Before running either, we tested the interview guide and survey, revising questions that could lead participants toward a particular answer and reordering others for a clearer flow.

03

Participants

Contact nurses were recruited through LinkedIn, hospital contact emails, professional Facebook groups, patient associations, and our own networks, alongside introductions via KI. This gave us in-depth interviews with three contact nurses working in different areas of cancer care, with nursing careers ranging from a few years to 30+ years, plus 8 survey responses. Specific role and workplace details are kept out to protect confidentiality.

3
Contact nurses interviewed

Individually, 45 minutes each.

8
Survey responses

From contact nurses in different roles.

04

Synthesis

We coded the three interviews into six categories and grouped the coded comments through affinity mapping. The survey results were mapped in alongside them, so the patterns that showed up in both the interviews and the survey stood out as the strongest.

05

Key findings

1. Support for relatives emerged as a gap. Support for patients’ relatives was the most selected unmet need in the survey (6 of 8 nurses). This finding came primarily from the survey, so it needs to be explored directly with patients and their families.

2. Nurses see both physical and psychological challenges in patients’ daily lives. Fatigue and anxiety, loneliness or poor mental health were each selected by 6 of 8 survey respondents. The interviews also described physical symptoms and emotional uncertainty.

3. Some support needs depend on the patient’s situation. The interviews raised gaps in psychological support, help returning to work, and access to sexual-health counselling for particular patient groups. The nurses also described differences in life situation, physical condition and personal outlook, as well as in digital ability, language and how patients take in information.

06

What we delivered to the client

The client asked us to conduct a needs analysis focused on healthcare staff in cancer care. We presented the findings above to KI, along with four recommendations for further investigation.

1. Investigate differences between patient groups. We recommended exploring whether different patient groups have different needs, and how that would affect the digital service, directly with patients.

2. Hear directly from patients. Our research captured nurses’ perspectives, so we recommended a patient survey to complement the findings.

3. Explore sexual-health support across more patient groups. The need surfaced in relation to particular groups; further research could show whether it applies more widely.

4. Consider accessible communication. We recommended paying attention to differences in digital ability, language and how patients take in information.

07

Limitations

Our findings reflect contact nurses’ perspectives, not patients’ own accounts. We therefore did not build patient personas based on these findings: doing so could turn indirect observations into an “average patient” who may not exist. This is also why we recommended speaking directly with patients as a next step.
